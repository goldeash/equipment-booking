**How a "Complete Diploma" Is Calculated**

The complexity criteria exist to help you verify that your project is sufficiently complex for a diploma. Think of them as a minimum threshold, not a guarantee:
- If your project does not meet the criteria → it will not be admitted to the defense (with rare exceptions).
- If your project meets the criteria → it satisfies the minimum complexity requirement, but admission also depends on implementation quality, defense performance, and expert evaluations.

Meeting all checkmarks is necessary but not sufficient for a successful defense.

| Level | Min. total checkmarks | Restrictions |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | 7 | You need at least 1 from each category (technical, data, infrastructure, processes) and 1 from required |
| Team | 10 | When working in a team, each student must choose their own 5 checkmarks |

Important: Checkmarks measure breadth (how many areas you cover). The pre-defense committee also evaluates depth (how complex each component is). A project with 7 checkmarks at surface level may still be deemed insufficient. Conversely, a highly specialized project (e.g., a Rust framework) with fewer UI components but deep backend complexity may be accepted — discuss with your supervisor early.

**What Is Not Allowed**

Violating any of these rules leads to topic rejection with no option to revise.

| Not Allowed | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Template online stores | Ready-made CMS themes (WooCommerce, Shopify, PrestaShop, etc.) with minimal changes to the interface or logic, and similar projects. |
| Reuse of previous work | Projects already defended in other subjects, courses, hackathons, or external competitions. |
| Tutorial clone | A step-by-step copy of a GitHub repository, article, or YouTube course without significant improvements. |
| Zero code originality | Auto-generated code/scripts where 80% or more of lines match public examples without original modifications. |
| Black-box Low-code | Built with website builders (Wix, Tilda, etc.) where you cannot show the source code or architecture. Also, using messengers (Telegram, etc.) as the front-end part of the application without significant custom logic. |
| License violations | Use of illegal software, unlisted OSS licenses, EULA violations. |
| No GDPR compliance | Projects that process personal data without a Privacy Notice and DPA. |
| Ethical restrictions | Content that violates academic ethics, promotes violence, or discrimination. |
| Low technical level | Work that does not meet the minimum score (see the criteria matrix). |

Whether a project falls into the "risk zone" is discussed during the topic approval stage.

### Supervisor Role

Stage 1 of 7 — Topic Selection | Weeks 1–2

Students: Understand what your supervisor does and what they expect from you.
Staff: Your responsibilities as a supervisor — weekly standups, checkpoints, thesis review.
Prerequisites: You are looking for or have been assigned a supervisor.

**Project Supervisor**

**Supervisor Qualifications**

Your project supervisor can be:
- An SDC instructor with an academic degree (Master's, Candidate of Technical Sciences, or Doctoral)
- An instructor from another university with an academic degree (Master's, Candidate of Technical Sciences, or Doctoral)
- A specialist from another organization with at least 3 years of relevant practical experience in the field of the specialty (in this case, an academic degree is not required)

**Supervisor Responsibilities During the Diploma Project**

*Choose a Topic Together with the Student*
- Verify that you have collected the required number of complexity points from the topic and complexity requirements.
- Sign the application form and record all agreements using the provided template.

*Set Up a Regular Communication Process*
- Hold weekly meetings with the student (standup format, at least 15 minutes).
- Topics: what was planned, what was done, reasons for delays, blockers, whether help is needed.
- Meetings must be documented in writing.
- Rescheduling by 1–2 days is acceptable.

*Track Progress*
- Conduct 4 progress reviews (опроцентовки) according to the established schedule (progress milestones).
- Make sure the student completes all required items for the chosen topic and complexity level on time.
- If needed, direct the student to expert consultations in specific areas (UI, Backend, Frontend, etc.).

*Review the Explanatory Note*
- Mandatory proofreading of the explanatory note in English.
- Check the structure, terminology, language, and logical flow.
- The student must send the final version of the explanatory note to the supervisor at least 5 working days before the pre-defense.
- The supervisor reviews and signs off on the submitted version. If the student makes changes after sign-off, a new review cycle is required.
- Versioning: The supervisor should confirm which version (by date or commit hash) was reviewed.

*Admission Decision*
- The supervisor decides on admission to the pre-defense:
- Signs the title page if the student is admitted.
- Provides a mandatory written explanation if the student is not admitted.
- Final admission to the defense is granted only after all required components (UI, Frontend, Backend, etc.) are approved by expert instructors.

### Topic Changes

Stage 1 of 7 — Topic Selection | Weeks 1–2

Students: Read this if you need to change your topic before or after approval.
Staff: Policy for handling topic change requests.
Prerequisites: You have started the topic selection process.

**Diploma Topics and Deadlines for Changes**

According to university regulations and branch office rules, you can change or adjust the topic at the following stages:

*Before the administrative deadline*

You can change the topic and complexity criteria until [date TBD by administration]. To do this, submit a new topic application using the standard form with the current date and fill in all the information again.

*After the BA defense*

After successfully defending the BA criterion before a committee, you may change your project topic — including switching to a completely different idea. If you change your topic, you must re-defend the BA criterion for the new topic. This is the last point where radical topic changes are allowed.

*After the pre-defense*

After the pre-defense (a preliminary committee review before the final defense), you may only make minor adjustments to the topic wording or description — within 3 working days, and only if the committee recommends changes. Radical changes to the topic, project scope, or project description are not allowed at this stage. The pre-defense confirms the direction of your project; from this point, you refine and finalize.

You must send all applications to office@esdc.lt. Always add your supervisor to the recipients.

## Раздел 3. Process and Timeline

### Diploma Lifecycle

Stage 2 of 7 — Topic Approval | Week 3

Students: Understand the full 6-stage lifecycle of your diploma project.
Staff: Reference for the complete process with all decision gates.
Prerequisites: You have chosen your topic and supervisor (Stage 1).

**Key Stages of the Diploma Project**

*Introduction*

This section describes the step-by-step diploma project process. You will learn about the roles of the supervisor and experts. You will also learn about the stages and actions needed for a successful defense.

For specific dates and week numbers, see Timeline.

*Choosing a Supervisor and Topic*
- You choose your supervisor.
- Together with your supervisor, you define the topic and the list of complexity items ("checkmarks").
- You fill in and sign the application form.

*Topic Approval*
- The application is submitted for approval by the administration.
- If the topic is not approved, you face automatic expulsion.

*Working with Your Supervisor*
- Weekly standup meetings with your supervisor (15 min, at least once a week).
- Reports follow the format: what was done, what is planned, blockers.
- You must pass 4 progress checkpoints (опроцентовки) — stages where your work is reviewed and accepted.
- At checkpoint 2 (midpoint), the supervisor must explicitly assess whether the project is on track to meet the required complexity level. If there is a risk that the project may not be complex enough for the diploma, the student must be informed in writing at this stage — not at pre-defense.
- If the supervisor identifies a complexity risk, the student must either: Add scope to increase complexity, or Adjust the topic (see Topic Changes)
- You must submit all complexity items to experts on time.
- You must prepare an explanatory note for your supervisor to review.

*Working with Expert Instructors*
- Each complexity item you chose requires a separate submission to an expert.
- You are required to:
  - Pick a date and time slot. Sign up for a meeting with the expert on the assigned days to defend the relevant section of your diploma.
  - Send the section materials to the expert at least one week before the meeting.
  - Attend the meeting with the expert to defend your work and receive your grade.
- You receive verbal feedback at the meeting with the expert.
- You may make changes based on the feedback. You may file an appeal no later than the date when the final grade is posted for that stage.

*BA Defense (Separate Committee Event)*
- The Business Analysis criterion is not assessed by an individual expert like other criteria.
- It requires a separate defense before a committee — this is the first formal gate in the diploma process.
- You must pass the BA defense before proceeding to other expert assessments.
- After passing the BA defense, you may change your project topic (see Topic Changes), but you must re-defend BA for the new topic.

*Pre-defense and Final Defense*

The pre-defense (предзащита) is a formal evaluation with a working committee. It carries the same weight as the final defense — treat it accordingly:
- You must have a deployed application, finished demo video, and complete presentation
- The committee evaluates your project's complexity, your ability to present, and your technical decisions
- A poor pre-defense can result in denial of admission to the final defense
- You may receive binding feedback that requires changes before the final defense

After a successful pre-defense, you receive permission to proceed to the final defense and a referral for review.
- You need to get 2 reviews — one from your supervisor and one from an external expert.
- The final defense takes place with a committee and external experts.

*Summary*

You must meet all deadlines, work with your supervisor regularly, and pass all components with experts. These are required conditions for admission to the defense. Missing deadlines or failing key items may lead to expulsion or denial of admission to the diploma defense.

Grades and other diploma project results (for example, weekly meeting attendance statistics) will be shared with the working and final committees as reference materials.

### Expert Assessment

Stage 4 of 7 — Build & Get Assessed | Weeks 7–15

Students: How expert assessments work — grading, meetings, and appeals.
Staff: Your procedures for running expert assessments.
Prerequisites: Topic approved. Development started. Ready to book assessment slots.

**Expert Instructor Responsibilities**

*Preparing Requirements and Report Format*
- Check the compliance checklist and the description of expected results for the discipline.
- Specify the report format that you must provide.
- Define the method for collecting materials (form, platform, deadlines).

*Assigning Slots*
- Set dates and slots for defending a part of the project (discipline) after topics are approved and according to the timeline.
- Give you the ability to choose a suitable time from the available slots.

*Pre-check*
- A few days before the meeting, check that you have submitted all required materials.
- If you have not submitted the materials, you are not allowed to be assessed.

*Individual Meetings*

The expert holds no more than two meetings with you:
- Stage defense: Assess how well you completed the item. Explain the grade verbally. Give recommendations on how to improve the grade before the final defense.
- Appeal: Assign the final grade. Communicate the decision. No further appeal is possible.

*Grading and Pass/Fail*

After the meeting:
- A grade on a 10-point scale is assigned.
- The item is marked as passed or not passed.
- If the grade is below 5 or the item is not passed: You are not admitted to the final diploma defense.

**Complexity Evaluation**

In addition to individual criterion grades, the pre-defense committee evaluates the overall project complexity. This considers:
- Depth of each component — a criterion implemented at a basic level contributes less to overall complexity than one implemented with advanced patterns
- Integration complexity — how components interact and whether the system demonstrates real distributed/full-stack behavior
- Novelty — whether the project goes beyond tutorial-level implementations

A project that passes all individual criteria at minimum level but lacks overall complexity may not be admitted to the final defense. The pre-defense committee makes this determination.

This applies particularly to non-standard projects (e.g., a framework or library with no frontend) where the depth of a single domain may compensate for missing breadth.

### Booking Slots

Stage 4 of 7 — Build & Get Assessed | Weeks 7–15

Students: How to book assessment slots with experts. Book early.
Staff: Slot availability rules and no-show policies.
Prerequisites: Expert assessment slots have been announced by administration.

**Rules for Booking a Discipline Review Slot with an Expert Instructor**

Booking a review slot is entirely your responsibility.

Instructors provide a fixed number of slots that matches the number of students in the group. Reviewing student work is not the main priority for instructors, who have their primary professional duties.

*Instructors Are Not Required To*
- Give up personal time outside working hours (before 9:00 or after 21:00)
- Create extra slots for students who missed the set deadlines
- Adjust their schedule to fit your individual preferences

*Example Situation*

For the "Artificial Intelligence" discipline, 17 slots were available for 17 students. Only 5 students signed up before the deadline.

Result: 12 slots of 30 minutes each went unused. When trying to find extra time, it turns out that students keep putting off sign-up. Then they try to book time at 8:00–9:00, ignoring the stated working hours.

*Important Warning*

There is a separate problem: some students book slots and do not show up without any notice. This is disrespectful to the instructor and blocks time for other students. If you signed up, you must attend or cancel your booking in advance.

### Business Analysis

Stage 3 of 7 — Build & Get Assessed | Weeks 4–18

Students: Mandatory criterion. Must be defended before a committee, not just an expert.
Staff: BA assessment rubric — committee evaluation, not individual expert.
Prerequisites: Topic approved. This is the first criterion to complete.

**Business Analysis Requirements for Passing the BA Stage of Diploma Preparation**

Status: Mandatory criterion with a separate defense. Unlike other criteria, Business Analysis requires a separate defense before a committee — not just an assessment with an individual expert. Passing this defense is a prerequisite for continuing with the diploma project.

Topic change policy: After successfully defending the BA criterion, you may change your project topic — this is the last point where radical topic changes are allowed. If you change your topic, you must re-defend the BA criterion for the new topic. After the pre-defense, only minor adjustments are permitted (see Topic Changes).

This document includes:
1. Minimum based requirements — requirements you must cover to pass the stage (grade — 5)
2. Maximum based requirements — requirements you are expected to cover to pass the stage with the highest grade (grade — 10).

**Minimum Based Requirements**

*Vision, Problem, Project*
a. Vision Statement (like an elevator pitch)
b. Problem Statement (Background/Context) — describe the problem your product aims to solve.
c. Business goals and objectives — goals and tasks you want to achieve.
d. Key stakeholders — key stakeholders, including the target audience and users.

*Scope*
a. In-Scope — what is included in the project (features, processes, systems).
b. Out-of-Scope — what will not be done, to avoid scope creep.

*High-Level Solution Overview*
a. Proposed Solution — a general description of the proposed technical solution.
b. Architecture / Integration Landscape (High-Level) — a diagram or text description of the systems involved and how they connect.

*Features & Requirements (High-Level)*
a. Core Features — a list of key capabilities (epics, capabilities).
  i. Functional Requirements - Work Breakdown: epics broken down into a skeleton-level list of user stories.
b. Non-Functional Requirements — non-functional requirements for the product (performance, security, scalability, availability).
c. Regulatory / Compliance Needs — legal and regulatory requirements (GDPR, ISO, etc.) that apply to the project.
d. Use Case Diagram — a visual representation of main usage scenarios (actors and their interactions with system features).

**Maximum Based Requirements**

*Introduction*
a. Purpose of the Document — why this document exists, its structure and goals (for example: agree on the project scope, give all parties a shared understanding).
b. Audience — who this document is written for.

*Vision*
a. Vision Statement (like an elevator pitch).
b. Problem Statement (Background/Context) — describe the business problem and the current situation.
c. Business Goals & Objectives — business goals and tasks you want to achieve (for example: reduce order processing time by 20%).
d. Stakeholders Analysis — analysis of stakeholders, including the target audience and users, and their influence/involvement (Influence/Interest matrix).
e. Success Criteria — measurable success criteria for the project (KPI, SLA, metrics).

*Scope*
a. In-Scope — what is included in the project (features, processes, systems).
b. Out-of-Scope — what will not be done, to avoid scope creep.
c. Assumptions — important assumptions the project is built on (for example: API availability, data readiness).
d. Constraints — limitations (budget, deadlines, technology, regulations, available resources).

*High-Level Solution Overview*
a. Proposed Solution — a general description of the proposed technical solution.
b. Architecture / Integration Landscape (High-Level) — a diagram or text description of the systems involved and how they connect.
c. Alternatives Considered — what options were considered and why this one was chosen.

*Features & Requirements*
a. Core Features — a list of key capabilities (epics, capabilities).
  i. Functional Requirements - Work Breakdown: epics broken down into a list of user stories.

### Microservices

Stage 3 of 7 — Build & Get Assessed | Weeks 4–18

Students: Grading rubric for microservices. Independent deployable services, async communication.
Staff: Assessment rubric for microservice architecture.
Prerequisites: Architecture decision to use microservices (justified).

**Microservice Architecture**

Notes and Context: Microservice architecture fits projects with clear separation of business domains and a need to scale components independently. Do not use microservices just for the sake of it — the architecture must solve specific problems in your project.

Important: You need at least 3 services to demonstrate a distributed system. Simply splitting frontend/backend/db does NOT count as microservice architecture.

**Minimum Requirements (5 points)**

Documentation — Required sections: Architecture diagram: all services, their interactions, protocols; Domain model: bounded contexts; API documentation: contracts between services (OpenAPI/AsyncAPI); Deployment diagram: how services are deployed; Decomposition rationale: why this specific split was chosen

Architecture:

Service separation: At least 3 business services (not counting infrastructure services); Each service is responsible for a separate business domain; Services can work independently (loose coupling); No shared database (each service has its own database or schema) — or a reason why sharing is necessary

Communication: Synchronous: REST API or gRPC between services; Clear interaction contracts (versioned APIs); Timeouts and retry policies for all calls; Handling of unavailable dependent services; Rate limiting on API Gateway or service entry points (basic request throttling to prevent cascade failures)

Implementation — Each service must: Have its own codebase (can be in a monorepo); Build into a separate Docker image (if the containerization criterion is chosen); Have an independent database or isolated schema; Provide health/ready endpoints; Log with a correlation ID for tracing

API Gateway or entry point: Single entry point for external clients; Request routing to services; Basic authentication/authorization; Rate limiting (requests per second/minute per client)

Error Handling: Graceful degradation when a service is unavailable; Return partial results where possible; Correct HTTP error codes; Centralized error logging

Testing: Unit tests for each service; Integration tests for critical flows; Contract testing between services

Restrictions — What you must not do: Synchronous call chains longer than 3 services; Direct access to another service's database; Shared libraries with business logic; Distributed transactions (2PC); A monolith that is only split into folders

**Maximum Requirements (10 points)**

All minimum requirements PLUS at least 2-3 directions:

Direction 1: Asynchronous Communication — Message broker (RabbitMQ, Kafka, NATS); Event-driven architecture with domain events; Pub/Sub pattern for loose coupling; Dead letter queues for failed messages; Handler idempotency; Event sourcing (optional)

Direction 2: Resilience Patterns — Circuit breakers (Hystrix pattern); Bulkheads for resource isolation; Rate limiting between services; Retry with exponential backoff; Timeout management; Fallback mechanisms

Direction 3: Observability — Distributed tracing (Jaeger/Zipkin); Correlation IDs across all services; Centralized logging (ELK/Loki); Metrics collection (Prometheus); Custom business metrics; Service dependency mapping

Direction 4: Advanced Patterns — Saga pattern for distributed transactions; CQRS for separating reads and writes; API composition pattern; Backend for Frontend (BFF); Service registry and discovery; Database per service with synchronization

Direction 5: API Management — Advanced API Gateway (Kong, Zuul); Rate limiting per client; API versioning strategy; Request/Response transformation; Caching at the gateway level; OAuth2/JWT authentication

**Recommendations**

When you do NOT need microservices: Simple CRUD application; No clear domain boundaries

Good decomposition examples: E-commerce: Orders, Inventory, Payments, Users, Notifications; Streaming: Content, Users, Recommendations, Analytics, Billing; Ride-sharing: Rides, Drivers, Passengers, Payments, Tracking; Food delivery: Restaurants, Orders, Delivery, Payments, Reviews

Anti-patterns to avoid: Entity services (UserService, ProductService); Services that are too small (CRUD for a single table); Services that are too large (mini-monoliths); Circular dependencies between services

- Simple proxy with no added value — Example: response = openai.complete(user_input) and nothing else. Minus 2 points
- No prompt engineering — Example: No system prompt, no context, no instructions. Minus 1 point

UX/Functionality Mistakes:
- No processing status indicator — Example: The user cannot tell if the bot is working. Minus 1 point
- Context loss within a session — Example: The bot forgets what was discussed 2 messages ago. Minus 1 point
- No input validation — Example: Messages of any length are accepted; the app crashes on emoji. Minus 1 point

Implementation Mistakes:
- Synchronous blocking processing — Example: The entire server waits for an OpenAI response for one user. Minus 1 point
- No logging — Example: It is impossible to trace problems in production. Minus 1 point
- Hardcoded configuration — Example: URLs, models, and parameters are hardcoded in the source. Minus 1 point

Documentation Mistakes:
- No prompt documentation — Example: It is unclear which prompts are used and why. Minus 1 point
- No description of system limitations — Example: It is not stated what the bot CANNOT do. Minus 1 point

Deployment Mistakes:
- Application is not deployed or not accessible — Example: Works only on localhost. Minus 2 points

**Recommendations**

For a successful defense: Choose a specific domain and make the assistant an expert in it; Show that you understand how LLMs work through good prompt engineering; Demonstrate several interesting use cases, not just Q&A; Always handle edge cases and errors; Add at least one standout feature from the maximum requirements

Examples of good topics: Medical assistant with RAG over medical reference books; Educational tutor with personalization based on the student's level; HR assistant integrated with corporate systems; E-commerce consultant with product catalog search; Game master for text-based RPGs with world state management

### Defense Preparation

Stage 6 of 7 — Pre-Defense | Week 19

Students: How to prepare for defense. Practice your pitch, deploy your app, prepare for Q&A.
Staff: What to expect from student presentations.
Prerequisites: All deliverables ready. Application deployed and accessible.

**Tips Before Attending the Defense (Pre-Defense)**

**Elevator Pitch**: Add an answer to this question on your first slide and at the start of your speech: "What does my application do — explained in 30 seconds to a person on the street? Why should they buy or use it?"

**Goal and Tasks**: State your goal and tasks clearly. The committee should understand the scope within the first minute.

**Database**: Fill your database with realistic data (not test/admin/123).

**Research Projects**: If your project is research-oriented (ML model, algorithm comparison, data analysis), you must present: Hypothesis or research question — what you set out to prove or investigate; Methodology — how you structured the experiment (dataset, parameters, conditions); Baseline — what the starting point or comparison benchmark was; Results with metrics — quantitative outcomes (accuracy, precision, F1, latency, throughput, etc.); Success/failure criteria — define in advance what constitutes a successful outcome vs. a failed one; Interpretation — what the results mean and whether they support the hypothesis.

A negative result (hypothesis disproven) is acceptable if the methodology was sound and the analysis is thorough. Do not hide negative results — present them honestly and explain what you learned.

**Deployment**: Your application must be deployed and accessible via a public URL before the pre-defense. This is a hard requirement, not optional. If your application is not deployed, you will not be admitted to the defense. Verify the URL works from a different network (not your local machine); Ensure the deployment will remain live through the final defense date; Have a backup plan (local demo) in case of cloud outage — but this does not replace the deployment requirement.

**Speech**: Practice your speech in English — out loud, with a timer.

**Thesis Document**: The thesis document (пояснительная записка) is your responsibility. If the automated generation does not work, create it manually in Word.

**Presentation Strategy**

*Focus on Strengths*: Lead with what you did well. The committee forms their impression in the first 2-3 minutes. If you open with apologies ("I didn't have time for...", "this part is incomplete..."), you set a negative frame that colors everything after it.

Do: Open with your elevator pitch and strongest feature; Show your best technical decisions with confidence; Present challenges as "Problem → Solution" stories; Acknowledge limitations briefly in the Results slide — don't dwell

Don't: Apologize for criteria you scored lower on; Say "I know this isn't great, but..."; Spend more time on what's missing than on what's done; Read your slides word for word

*Time Allocation*: Spend 70% of your time on your strongest 3-4 criteria and the product demo. Spend 30% on the remaining criteria. The committee will ask about weak areas in Q&A — that is the appropriate place to discuss limitations.
